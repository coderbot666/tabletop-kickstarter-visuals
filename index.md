# Planning Visuals for a Tabletop Game Kickstarter Page

A crowdfunding page is mostly read by scrolling. Backers decide in the first screen whether to keep going. Here is the visual plan I put together for a small card game campaign.

## The order of images
1. **Hero banner:** the game box and cards on a table, with the title. If you have no box yet, a clean 3D render is acceptable, but label it as a prototype.
2. **What's in the box:** a flat-lay of every component.
3. **How to play in three panels:** setup, a turn, scoring.
4. **Art showcase:** a few card illustrations at full size.
5. **Stretch goals and timeline.**

## Concept art vs final art
Early on we used generated images to explore the look of the world: for instance "floating island markets, airships, painterly style" made with [seedream5-5.net](https://seedream5-5.net/). This helped the team agree on a palette before commissioning the artist. On the campaign page itself we only showed the artist's final illustrations, and we stated clearly who drew them. Backers ask about this now, and being upfront builds trust.

## Make text readable on phones
Most traffic comes from phones. Text baked into images should be at least 40 px tall at the page width. Long rules explanations belong in real text, not in images.

## GIFs, sparingly
One short GIF of a card being played helps. Five of them slow the page and distract.

## Final check
Scroll the page on a small phone with a slow connection. If the first image takes more than a few seconds to load, compress it.
